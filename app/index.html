Scout the ground before you burn the gas.

Draw a box. Transect reads terrain, hydrography, land cover, burn history and rut timing inside it, ranks the ground worth hunting, and tells you why — then filters the whole plan against how far you'll actually walk and how the meat comes out.

Built for one hunt in northern Québec and opened up in case someone else wants it. No subscription, no upsell, no roadmap.

How it works
01

Draw your box

Search a lake or drag a box on the map. Set your hunt dates — they drive rut phase, weather and which behaviour the model weights.

02

Say who's hunting

Boat or no boat, spike camp or back to the truck, how far you'll walk. These aren't cosmetic — with no boat, rivers become foot barriers and ground across one drops out.

03

The engine runs

Three to five minutes. It pulls a DEM, satellite land cover, mapped fire history and hydrography for your box, then scores habitat against retrievability and pressure.

04

Take it to the bush

Ranked areas with the reasoning, calling sites keyed to wind, camps, pack-out maths, and a GPX/KML export that opens in OnX or a Garmin.

The one rule

Never render data it doesn't have.

A layer with nothing behind it reads NO DATA rather than drawing a plausible line. Inferences are labelled as inferences. Scores say what they're relative to. Where the evidence is thin, the interface says so instead of choosing quietly.

Every mark it makes is a hypothesis to ground-truth on foot. À valider sur le terrain.

⃠
It won't find you a moose
It models habitat, access and timing. Animals are not in the data.
NOT A GUARANTEE
▲
Wallows aren't predicted
There's no published method for locating them from terrain. Anyone claiming otherwise is guessing.
NO METHOD
▲
Cutblock age is missing
Burn history is mapped; logging regeneration isn't, so it's under-counted.
FIRE ONLY
◔
It isn't validated yet
No harvest or survey data has been used to test whether it beats a simple distance-to-road rule.
UNPROVEN
◔
It's a desk tool
No offline mode. The field artifact is the GPX you export before you lose signal.
PLAN AT HOME
It's a hobby project, and the code says so.
A Python engine and a MapLibre front end, no build step. Fork it, point it at your own zone, or just read how the habitat model scores ground. Issues welcome; promises not offered.
